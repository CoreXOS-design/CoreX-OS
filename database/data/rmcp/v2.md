# Risk Management and Compliance Programme — Version 2

Prepared in terms of section 42 of the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001.

{{agency.name}} · FIC registration number {{agency.fic_no}} · PPRA FFC {{agency.ffc_no}}

## 1. Definitions

- **FICA / the Act:** the Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, as amended, including the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022, its Regulations, Directives and Guidance Notes.
- **FIC:** the Financial Intelligence Centre established under chapter 2 of FICA.
- **RMCP:** this Risk Management and Compliance Programme, required by section 42 of FICA.
- **Company:** {{agency.name}}, including all branches, agents and staff.
- **Client:** any seller, buyer, owner (landlord) or tenant with whom the Company establishes a business relationship or concludes a single transaction, including any person acting on the client's behalf and any beneficial owner of the client.
- **Business relationship:** an arrangement for an ongoing relationship, for example a rental management mandate or a lease.
- **Single transaction:** a once-off transaction of R5 000 or more, for example a sale.
- **CDD:** customer due diligence — identifying and verifying the client and understanding the relationship.
- **EDD:** enhanced due diligence applied to high-risk clients.
- **Beneficial owner:** the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a legal person, partnership or trust.
- **DPIP:** domestic politically exposed person (Schedule 1). **FPPO:** foreign politically exposed person (Schedule 2).
- **RO:** a risk / FICA officer appointed by the Company to review FICA. **CO:** the FICA Compliance Officer (section 28).
- **OTP:** Offer to Purchase. **OATS / EATS:** open / exclusive authority to sell or let.
- **FICA form (wet ink):** the paper FICA questionnaire a client completes and signs with the OTP.
- **Online FICA:** the electronic FICA questionnaire sent to the client by secure link from CoreX OS.
- **CoreX OS:** the property management system in which the Company records, reviews and stores all FICA.
- **TFS:** targeted financial sanctions; the FIC TFS list at tfs.fic.gov.za.
- **STR / CTR / TPR:** suspicious and unusual transaction report (s29), cash threshold report (s28), terrorist property report (s28A).
- **TVA:** The Virtual Agent. **Lightstone:** Lightstone Property. **CMA:** CMA Info.

## 2. Introduction

As property practitioners the Company is an accountable institution under Schedule 1 of FICA. This RMCP sets out how the Company identifies, assesses, monitors, mitigates and manages the risk that its services are used for money laundering, terrorist financing or proliferation financing. Money laundering risk in residential property is lower than in many sectors, but property is a recognised channel for laundering, and the Company applies this RMCP in all its business.

## 3. Our business services

- Listing and selling residential and commercial property (sellers and buyers).
- Listing and letting residential and commercial property for 3 months or longer, including rental management (owners and tenants).
- The Company does not accept cash, does not hold purchase funds (these are paid to the appointed conveyancer), and receives rental and deposit funds by EFT only into its trust account.

## 4. Risk assessment

The Company rates every client Low, Medium or High risk using the four factors below. Medium is the starting rating. The agent proposes the rating in CoreX OS and the RO / CO confirms or changes it at approval. The reason for the rating is recorded on the FICA file.

| Factor | Lower risk | Higher risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Client** | SA citizen or resident natural person, documents complete and consistent, verified through TVA / Lightstone | DPIP / FPPO, family member or close associate; trust or company with unclear ownership; reluctant to give information; possible TFS match |
| **Service** | Long-term letting at market rent; sale through an appointed conveyancer with bond finance | Cash offers; rent paid far in advance; quick resale; price far from market value; payments from or to unrelated third parties |
| **Delivery channel** | Client met in person and original ID seen | Client never met (online FICA, foreign or remote client) — extra verification step in section 8 applies |
| **Geography** | Client and funds in South Africa | Client, funds or owners linked to a FATF high-risk or increased-monitoring jurisdiction, or with no presence in South Africa |

**New services and technology:** before the Company introduces a new service, delivery channel or technology (for example a change to online FICA or CoreX OS), the CO assesses its money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risk and updates this RMCP where needed.

**Business profile (update annually):** area of operation {{agency.area_of_operation}}; average sales per year {{profile.avg_sales_per_year}}; average monthly rent per lease {{profile.avg_monthly_rent}}; leases under management {{profile.active_leases}}; branches {{agency.branches_statement}}.

## 5. Our risks

- A buyer or tenant uses money from an illegal source to buy or rent property.
- A tenant uses the property for a business that launders money or finances terrorism.
- An owner uses sale or rental income to finance terrorism or proliferation.
- A client hides their identity, or the real owner hides behind a company, trust or representative.
- A client never met in person uses someone else's identity (online FICA).
- A transaction finances the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction.

This list is updated whenever new risks are identified.

## 6. Who we apply CDD to

The Company performs CDD on every client and prospective client:

- **Sellers:** when the property is listed (OATS or EATS).
- **Owners (landlords):** when the property is listed for letting.
- **Buyers:** when the OTP is completed.
- **Tenants:** when the rental application is submitted.
- **Representatives and principals:** any person acting for a client, and the person a client acts for, including proof of authority.
- **Beneficial owners:** of every legal person, partnership and trust (sections 12–14).

CDD also applies to existing clients from before this RMCP.

## 7. When CDD must be done

Identity information and documents are obtained at or before signature of the mandate, OTP, rental application or lease. Verification and RO / CO approval are completed during the course of concluding the transaction and before it is given further effect — in particular before keys are handed over or the lease starts, and before the sale proceeds to registration. If CDD cannot be completed, section 17 applies.

## 8. How we obtain and verify FICA

All FICA is recorded in CoreX OS, whichever route it comes in by.

**Route 1 — wet-ink FICA (sales).** Buyers and sellers complete and sign the paper FICA form with the OTP. The agent sees the original ID document at signing, then loads the form and documents into CoreX OS.

**Route 2 — online FICA.** CoreX OS emails the client a secure, time-limited link. The client completes the questionnaire for their type (natural person, company / close corporation, trust or partnership), uploads their documents and signs electronically. Answers, documents and the signature are stored on the FICA file.

**Documents required.** ID or passport; proof of residential address not older than 3 months; for legal persons, partnerships and trusts the documents in sections 12–14; proof of authority where someone acts for another.

**Verification.** The agent checks the documents against the client's details and verifies ID and address through TVA and / or Lightstone (and ownership through Lightstone / CMA for sellers and owners).

**Clients not met in person.** Where the client was not met face to face, the agent must confirm the client's identity by at least one of the following before approving, and record which one in CoreX OS:

- met in person later and saw the original ID;
- video call (including WhatsApp video) in which the client shows the original ID;
- certified copies of the ID document and proof of address.

**Approval.**

1. Agent review: checklist, verification method, TFS result and proposed risk rating.
2. RO / CO review and approval with signature. An RO may not approve a FICA they requested or reviewed at stage 1. High-risk clients are approved only by the CO with senior management (section 22).
3. CoreX OS records every step (who, what, when) in a history that cannot be edited.

## 9. Purpose and intended nature of the relationship (s21A)

For every client the Company records the purpose of the transaction (sell, buy, let, rent) and the intended nature of the relationship, how the transaction will be funded and the source of funds. For high-risk clients the source of wealth is also established.

## 10. Ongoing due diligence (s21C, s21D)

The Company monitors continuing relationships (owners and tenants) and checks that activity matches what it knows about the client. Warning signs include:

- an owner asking that rent be paid to an unknown third party without good reason;
- a tenant whose known income has stopped but who keeps paying;
- unverified changes to bank details;
- payment patterns that differ materially from what was expected;
- any other unexplained change in behaviour.

FICA for continuing relationships is reviewed at least every 12 months, and every 6 months for high-risk clients. A review is also triggered by a change of bank details, address, ownership or control, or any material change. Where the Company doubts the accuracy of information previously obtained, it verifies the client's information again (s21D).

## 11. Anonymous clients (s20A)

The Company may not deal with an anonymous client or a client using an apparently false or fictitious name. An employee who breaches this rule commits an offence and faces disciplinary action.

## 12. Legal persons (companies and close corporations)

The Company establishes and verifies: the name, registration number and registered address; the nature of the business; the ownership and control structure; and each beneficial owner — every natural person who directly or indirectly holds 5% or more of the shares or voting rights. If no one qualifies, or there is doubt, the natural persons who exercise control by other means; if still none, the senior managing official. Documents: CIPC registration documents, directors' list, shareholding, resolution authorising the representative, and ID and proof of address of each beneficial owner and representative.

## 13. Partnerships

The Company establishes and verifies the partnership name; every partner, including silent partners; the person with executive control; and each person authorised to bind the partnership.

## 14. Trusts

The Company establishes and verifies the trust name and registration number; the Master's office where it is registered; the founder; each trustee and each person authorised to act for the trust; each named beneficiary or, if none, how beneficiaries are determined; and any other natural person who exercises effective control. Documents: letters of authority, trust deed, trustees' resolution, and IDs and proof of address.

## 15. Complex or unusually large transactions

Complex or unusually large transactions, or unusual patterns with no apparent business or lawful purpose, receive EDD and are referred to the CO.

## 16. Unusual patterns in rentals and sales

Staff watch for: unusual purposes for the property; rent or price far from market; rent paid far in advance; requests for refunds of money already paid; business use that cannot support the rent; and payments from or to unrelated third parties.

## 17. Inability to conduct CDD (s21E)

If CDD cannot be completed, the Company may not establish the relationship, conclude the transaction or perform any act to give effect to it, and must end any existing relationship in line with this RMCP. Senior management informs the client in writing. The CO considers whether an STR must be filed.

## 18. Targeted financial sanctions (s26A–26C, s28A)

No one at the Company may deal with a person or entity on the TFS list, or with their property.

**Who is screened:** every client, every person acting for a client, and every director, member, trustee, partner and beneficial owner of a client, before the relationship or transaction, and staff (Schedule 8).

**How:** CoreX OS screens the client against the FIC list automatically. For a company or close corporation, the directors / members are loaded on the company record and each is screened as well. Any connected person not loaded in CoreX OS (for example a trustee, partner, beneficial owner who is not a director, or a representative) is screened by the agent on tfs.fic.gov.za and the result saved to the FICA file before the agent review. A client passes TFS screening only when the client and every connected person have passed.

**List updates:** the CO subscribes to FIC TFS notifications. When the list changes, the CO checks new names against the client database in CoreX OS without delay and records the check.

**Possible match (name or ID):** all activity stops and the matter is referred to the CO at once. The CO decides whether it is a false positive (reason recorded) or a confirmed match.

**Confirmed match:** no further dealing; the property or funds are not released or transferred; the CO files a TPR through goAML as soon as possible and within the prescribed period, informs senior management, and does not tip off the client.

## 19. Record keeping (s22–24)

- **What:** identity and verification records, FICA answers and documents, TFS results, risk ratings with reasons, approvals, transaction records and reports made.
- **Where:** electronically in CoreX OS (encrypted, with an unalterable history), with wet-ink originals on the transaction file.
- **How long:** at least 5 years from the end of the business relationship or the conclusion of the single transaction; records relating to a report to the FIC, at least 5 years from the date of the report, or longer if the FIC requests.
- **Protection:** no FICA record may be removed or destroyed early, or disclosed to a third party without the client's consent unless the law requires it, and never without CO authority.
- **Third-party storage:** CoreX OS (RR Technologies (Pty) Ltd) keeps the electronic records for the Company. The Company remains responsible, ensures records are available to the FIC and the PPRA (as supervisory body) without delay, and gives the FIC the prescribed particulars of the third party (s24(3)).

## 20. Duty to report suspicious and unusual transactions (s29)

Every staff member must report to the CO immediately, and in full, any transaction they know about or suspect, or ought reasonably to suspect, where:

- a party is receiving or using the proceeds of unlawful activity;
- property connected to money laundering, terrorist financing or proliferation financing is changing hands;
- the transaction has no apparent business or lawful purpose;
- the transaction is structured to avoid a FICA reporting duty;
- it relates to tax evasion or to money laundering or terrorist financing.

Attempted and abandoned transactions are also reported.

## 21. Confidentiality and tipping-off

No one may disclose a report, or the fact that a report is being considered, to the client or anyone else, except as FICA allows, for legal proceedings or by court order.

## 22. Prominent persons (s21F–21H)

Every client is asked whether they, a family member or a known close associate is a DPIP or FPPO (Schedules 1–3). A known close associate is a person known to have a close business or personal relationship with a DPIP or FPPO.

- FPPO, their family and close associates: always high risk.
- DPIP, their family and close associates: high risk where the CDD shows higher risk.

For these high-risk clients: senior management approval before the relationship starts or continues; source of wealth and source of funds established; enhanced ongoing monitoring.

## 23. Levels of CDD and high-risk clients

- Low and Medium: standard CDD (sections 8–14).
- High: EDD — CO and senior management approval, source of funds and wealth, extra verification, monitoring at least every 6 months. EDD steps are set case by case by the CO.

A client is high risk where any of these apply: FPPO, or DPIP with higher risk; client, funds or owners linked to a FATF high-risk or increased-monitoring jurisdiction; no residence, operations or premises in South Africa; complex ownership where the beneficial owner is unclear; cannot or will not give the required information without good reason; a party to an unusual or complicated transaction; a possible TFS match pending clearance; or any other reason the agent or CO considers suspicious. Being a foreign national is not on its own a reason for a high rating.

A transaction under R5 000 is not a single transaction requiring full CDD, but TFS screening still applies to every person.

**Cash:** the Company does not accept cash. Any cash offer is reported to the CO, who considers an STR. If the Company receives or pays cash above R49 999.99 (in one amount or in linked amounts) it files a CTR within the prescribed period.

## 24. Using another institution's CDD

Where a bank or conveyancer has done CDD on the same client for the same transaction, the Company may use their confirmation and documents, but remains responsible for its own CDD. It obtains the information without delay, checks it meets this RMCP, fills any gap with its own CDD, and still performs its own TFS screening and risk rating.

## 25. Reports to the FIC

Reports are filed by the CO on goAML (goaml.fic.gov.za). Credentials are held by the CO and a back-up authorised by senior management.

| Report | When | Deadline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STR / SAR (s29) | Knowledge or suspicion | As soon as possible, within 15 working days |
| CTR (s28) | Cash received above R49 999.99 | Within 3 working days |
| TPR (s28A) | Property of a TFS-listed person or entity | As soon as possible, within 5 working days |

The CO keeps an internal register of every report made by staff, the CO's decision and reasons, and the goAML reference.

## 26. Annual FIC submissions

- **RMCP (Directive 12):** the board-approved RMCP is submitted on goAML every year by 31 October, and any amended RMCP within 10 days of approval.
- **Risk and compliance return (Directive 11 / annual):** submitted by the date the FIC sets.
- **Registration:** FIC registration details kept up to date, with changes notified within the prescribed period.

## 27. Governance and approval (s42(2A), s42A)

This RMCP and every amendment is approved by the board of directors or, where there is no board, the senior management or highest authority of the Company. The board / senior management is responsible for ensuring compliance with FICA and this RMCP, appoints the CO, and co-operates with inspections by the FIC and the PPRA.

## 28. FICA Compliance Officer and branches

**Compliance Officer:** {{compliance_officer.full_name}} (ID {{compliance_officer.id_number}}). The CO:

- keeps the FIC registration up to date;
- implements this RMCP at the main office and every branch;
- appoints, with management, a responsible person at each branch: {{agency.branches_and_responsible}} (the Company has no branches or subsidiaries outside South Africa);
- trains staff and keeps the training register;
- reviews and approves FICA, decides TFS matches and files reports;
- keeps the report register and reports to senior management at least annually.

## 29. Training and staff acknowledgement (s43)

All staff receive FICA training within 30 days of starting and a refresher every year. Each staff member receives this RMCP and signs the staff acknowledgement (Annexure B) for every version.

## 30. Offences and penalties

Non-compliance is a disciplinary offence and may lead to dismissal. Under FICA, the FIC may impose administrative sanctions of up to R10 million for a natural person and R50 million for a legal person. Criminal offences carry fines of up to R100 million or imprisonment of up to 15 years for the most serious offences, such as failing to report a suspicious transaction.

## 31. Review of this RMCP

This RMCP is reviewed at least once a year before the 31 October submission, and also whenever risks, services, systems or the law change. The review date is recorded below.

## Schedule 1. Domestic politically exposed persons (DPIPs)

A person who holds, including in an acting position for more than six months, or has held at any time in the preceding 12 months, in the Republic:

- the President or Deputy President;
- a government minister or deputy minister;
- the Premier of a province or a member of a provincial Executive Council;
- an executive mayor, mayor, deputy mayor or municipal manager, or a municipal chief financial officer;
- a leader of a registered political party;
- a member of a royal family or a senior traditional leader;
- the head, accounting officer or chief financial officer of a national or provincial department;
- the chairperson, CEO, accounting authority, CFO or chief investment officer of a public entity in Schedule 2 or 3 of the PFMA, or of a municipal entity;
- a constitutional court judge or any other judge;
- an ambassador, high commissioner or other senior representative of a foreign government based in the Republic;
- an officer of the SANDF above the rank of major-general;
- the chairperson of the board, chairperson of the audit committee, executive officer or CFO of a company providing goods or services to an organ of state above the amount the Minister determines;
- the head, or an executive directly accountable to the head, of an international organisation based in the Republic.

## Schedule 2. Foreign politically exposed persons (FPPOs)

A person who holds, or has held at any time in the preceding 12 months, a prominent public function in a foreign country, including: head of state or government; member of a royal family; minister, senior politician or political party leader; senior judicial official; senior executive of a state-owned corporation; high-ranking military officer.

## Schedule 3. Family members and known close associates

Family members of a DPIP or FPPO include: spouse, civil or life partner; previous spouse, civil or life partner; children and step-children and their spouses or partners; parents; siblings and step-siblings and their spouses or partners.

A known close associate is a person known to have a close business or personal relationship with a DPIP or FPPO, including joint ownership of a legal person, partnership or trust.

## Schedules 4–7. FICA questionnaires

The questionnaires for natural persons (4), companies and close corporations (5), partnerships (6) and trusts (7) are issued as the wet-ink FICA form and the online FICA in CoreX OS. They cover identity, persons acting for the client, purpose of the transaction, source of funds, beneficial ownership and prominent-person status (sections 21, 21A, 21B and 21F–21H). Current versions are kept by the CO.

## Schedule 8. Employee vetting

- **Before appointment:** verify ID, address (proof not older than 3 months), criminal record (police clearance), credit check, PPRA registration and valid FFC, qualifications and references; screen against the TFS list.
- **Ongoing:** TFS re-screening and FFC check every year, and on every TFS list update; re-verify if circumstances change materially; report any suspicious behaviour.
- **Records:** kept on the employee file for 5 years after employment ends.
- **Training:** initial FICA training within 30 days, annual refresher, signed acknowledgement per session, training register kept by the CO.

## Annexure A. CDD steps per transaction type

**Risk rating:** every client starts at Medium. Low when the client is a South African citizen or resident, is not a prominent person, documents are complete and TVA / Lightstone verification matches. High per section 23. A high rating means EDD and CO / senior management approval; it is not on its own a reason to report to the FIC. Reports are made only under section 25.

### Sales — seller

1. Agent draws a Lightstone report and confirms ownership.
2. Seller signs the mandate and provides ID and proof of address (wet ink or online FICA).
3. ID and address verified through TVA; documents loaded in CoreX OS.
4. TFS screening of the seller and, for a company, its directors in CoreX OS; any other connected person on tfs.fic.gov.za (section 18).
5. Agent review, then RO / CO approval.
6. Discrepancies referred to the CO and the client re-rated.

### Sales — buyer

1. Buyer completes the wet-ink FICA form with the OTP; agent sees the original ID.
2. Purpose and source of funds recorded (bond, cash from own funds, sale of property, other).
3. ID and address verified through TVA and / or Lightstone; form and documents loaded in CoreX OS.
4. TFS screening as above.
5. Agent review, then RO / CO approval.
6. Discrepancies referred to the CO and the client re-rated.

### Letting — owner

1. Property listed; owner signs the mandate and completes FICA (online or wet ink).
2. Ownership confirmed through Lightstone / CMA; ID and address verified through TVA.
3. Bank account for rental payments confirmed in the owner's name.
4. TFS screening, agent review, RO / CO approval.
5. Reviewed at least every 12 months (6 months if high risk) and on any trigger in section 10.

### Letting — tenant

1. Tenant submits the rental application with ID, proof of address, income and bank statements.
2. Income and bank statements checked against the declared occupation or business.
3. Details verified through TVA; FICA completed and loaded in CoreX OS.
4. TFS screening, agent review, RO / CO approval.
5. The lease prohibits business use of residential premises without written consent.
6. Reviewed at least every 12 months (6 months if high risk) and on any trigger in section 10.
